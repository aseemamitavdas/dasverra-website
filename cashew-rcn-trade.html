Price & Specification

Why are cashew prices so different?

A cashew kernel's price is not determined simply by "cashew." It depends on the specification the buyer is actually purchasing. A premium whole-kernel grade can command a very different value from a smaller, lower-grade or broken kernel, driven by origin, crop quality, nut count, outturn, moisture, supply availability, processing recovery, season, freight, currency and destination demand.

Price without specification is not meaningful.

RCN → Kernel Economics

A processor does not evaluate RCN simply by looking for the lowest purchase price. The commercial question is what the RCN can produce after processing and what the resulting kernels can realise in the target market.

Know Your Cashew

Know your cashew

Dasverra's cashew market education series.

01Trade Education

Why W320 Is Not The Only Cashew Grade

W320 is one of the most widely traded whole-kernel grades, but it is only one part of the cashew market. Commercial trade also includes larger and smaller whole-kernel grades, splits, butts, pieces and other specifications.

02Trade Education

W180 vs W320 vs W400

The number is associated with kernel count per pound under the relevant grading convention. Lower count generally indicates a larger kernel. Commercial value also depends on quality, colour, defects, origin, availability and buyer specification.

03Trade Education

What Does SW Mean?

SW generally refers to Scorched Wholes: whole kernels with a darker appearance associated with processing, further classified by size and quality specification.

04Trade Education

Why Whole Kernels Command Different Prices

Whole kernels are not all commercially equivalent. Grade, size, colour, uniformity, defects, moisture, processing quality and market demand can create significant differences in value.

05Trade Education

Why RCN Origin Matters

Origin can influence crop characteristics, quality, availability, outturn, logistics, seasonality and processing economics. A processor evaluates the economics of the RCN rather than looking only at its purchase price.

06Trade Education

What Is Outturn?

Outturn is an important commercial measure used when evaluating RCN. It relates to the expected kernel yield from the raw nuts and helps processors evaluate the economics of procurement.

07Trade Education

How Processors Evaluate RCN

Processors may consider origin, nut count, moisture, outturn, quality, crop condition, availability, expected processing recovery, logistics and expected kernel realisation.

08Trade Education

Why A Cheap RCN Price May Not Mean Cheap Processing

A lower RCN purchase price does not automatically mean better economics. Quality, recovery, logistics and the value of the resulting kernel mix can change the commercial outcome.

09Trade Education

Africa → India Cashew Economics

The commercial equation can include RCN quality, outturn, processing economics, inland logistics, port costs, ocean freight, insurance, currency, financing, duties and destination requirements.

10Trade Education

Why Supply Consistency Matters More Than One Good Shipment

One shipment can demonstrate that a transaction is possible. Consistent specification, availability and execution are what make a repeatable supply chain.

11Trade Education

How Seasonality Affects RCN

RCN availability can be influenced by harvest periods, crop conditions, local supply, farmer selling patterns, inventories and demand. Understanding the supply cycle is important when planning procurement.

12Trade Education

Why Specification Should Come Before Price

Price without specification is not meaningful. Before comparing offers, the buyer needs to understand origin, grade, quality parameters, quantity, packaging, shipment terms, delivery point and payment structure.
